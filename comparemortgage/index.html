There are actually two different deadlines to distinguish:

1. Ontario's minimum disclosure/review period

For a mortgage arranged through an Ontario mortgage brokerage, required disclosures generally must be provided at least two business days before you enter into the mortgage agreement or sign the mortgage instrument, whichever comes first. Ontario defines business days as Monday–Friday excluding statutory holidays. FSRA+1

For your August 26 closing:

  • Wednesday, Aug. 26 — closing

  • Tuesday, Aug. 25 — 1 business day before

  • Monday, Aug. 24 — 2 business days before

  • Friday, Aug. 21 — 3 business days before

  • Thursday, Aug. 20 — 4 business days before

So, if the commitment agreement were going to be entered into on the closing date itself, the two-business-day period would point to Friday, August 21 as the latest day the required disclosure should ordinarily have been provided, absent a written waiver/reduction. Ontario

But that's not the deadline I'd recommend using.

Your practical deadline should be much earlier

For an August 26 closing, I would ideally want to have:

Decision made → Commitment signed → Lender conditions satisfied → Lawyer has mortgage instructions → Funds confirmed

by roughly August 17–19, rather than waiting until August 24–26.

Why?

Because the Commitment Letter isn't necessarily an unconditional promise that the money will be available on August 26.

FSRA specifically says that a commitment letter will usually contain conditions that must be met before the mortgage can actually complete, and that changes in your financial circumstances or documentation can potentially result in renegotiation or cancellation. FSRA

FSRA also says that you should receive an official lender-signed Mortgage Approval/Letter of Commitment with the conditions and terms clearly outlined. FSRA

One thing I'd check immediately

Look at each Commitment Letter for something like:

This commitment expires on August XX, 2026.

or:

This commitment is valid until...

or:

The mortgage must be funded by...

That date can be more important than the Ontario two-business-day disclosure period.

For example, if one lender's commitment says:

Commitment expires August 19, 2026

then you can't simply wait until August 24 to make your decision.

Conversely, if it says:

Commitment expires August 28, 2026

you have more flexibility.

And there's an important distinction between "signing the commitment" and "closing"

You don't necessarily need to wait until closing to sign the Commitment Letter. In fact, you generally shouldn't.
