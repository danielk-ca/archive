7. What happens if you want to refinance?

This needs to be divided into two situations.

Refinancing with the same lender

If you're simply replacing the existing mortgage before maturity with another mortgage from the same lender, Think's commitment specifically addresses early renewal.

For a variable-rate mortgage, Think says you can:

  • renew early into another 5-year variable mortgage, but pay the applicable payout penalty, or

  • lock into the then-current fixed rate without a payout penalty. True North Mortgage Commitment

That's actually a fairly valuable option.

Refinancing with another lender

That's effectively a payout/discharge of the SBI or Think mortgage.

Therefore, during the term, the prepayment rules become important.

For Think's variable mortgage, the commitment explicitly says that an early repayment beyond the privilege incurs three months' interest at the current mortgage rate, plus any applicable pro-rated rebate/penalty-covered amount. True North Mortgage Commitment

Your rebate is $0, so that latter component appears to be zero.

SBI similarly specifies three months' interest on the amount prepaid. SBI Mortgage Commitment

Therefore neither commitment gives you a penalty-free right to refinance to another lender during the five-year term.

At maturity, however, Think explicitly says the mortgage becomes fully open to repay, renew or move to another institution. True North Mortgage Commitment

SBI's maturity provision says the loan ends September 1, 2031 and, absent renewal, the outstanding balance is payable. SBI Mortgage Commitment

8. Porting to another house — Think wins clearly on the document

This is probably the most important difference if you think there's a reasonable chance you'll move during the five years.

Think

The commitment expressly provides Rights of Assumption and Portability.

For portability:

  • you must not be in default;

  • you must qualify under the lender's requirements at the time;

  • you must apply in writing;

  • a new mortgage is registered on the new property;

  • if the new mortgage is the same amount as the transferable principal, it gets the same rate for the remaining term;

  • if you borrow less, the excess is subject to the prepayment provisions;

  • if you borrow more, the additional amount gets current rates or a blended rate. True North Mortgage Commitment

There is, however, an important caveat for your specific mortgage.

Because you're putting 25% down, this is not a high-ratio insured mortgage. Think's own commitment says that for a non-high-ratio mortgage there is a chance the mortgage won't be portable, and the port request has to be assessed; it may also result in a new fee. True North Mortgage Commitment

So I would characterize Think as:

"Contractual portability mechanism, but not an unconditional portability guarantee for your uninsured mortgage."

The listed port fee is $495. True North Mortgage Commitment

SBI

SBI's commitment does not contain a comparable detailed portability provision that I found.

It does say:

"This mortgage can be assumed subject to our consent, approval of a qualified purchaser and agreement between you and us on any amended or supplemental terms that we may require." SBI Mortgage Commitment

That's an assumption clause, not a portability clause.

So if you sell House A and buy House B, I would not assume from this commitment that SBI will port the mortgage.

That is one of the biggest things I'd want SBI to confirm in writing.

10. What if rates rise?

This is another major difference.

Think: payment adjusts

Think says that when Prime changes:

  • the new rate takes effect immediately;

  • the next mortgage payment doesn't change;

  • the payment after that changes to reflect the new rate. True North Mortgage Commitment

So your payment is designed to respond to rate changes.

That means rising rates generally result in higher payments rather than silently extending the amortization as aggressively.

SBI: payment does not automatically track the rate in the same way

SBI explicitly warns that variable-rate changes will:

"increase or decrease the amortization period, the cost of borrowing and the balance, unless you adjust your Regular Principal and Interest Payment."

And SBI has a specific trigger at 5.54% in the existing $372,000 commitment: above that rate, the stated payment isn't enough even to cover the interest, creating the possibility of negative amortization. SBI can then require an increased payment. SBI Mortgage Commitment

That's an important risk.

At 5.54%:

SBI's current $1,716.68 payment would no longer fully cover interest on the $372,000 loan.

Because you're considering increasing the down payment to 25%, the actual payment and trigger would presumably be different. The current commitment doesn't give us the revised $348,750 trigger rate.

This is one thing I'd want SBI to put in the revised commitment.

11. What if rates fall?

Think

The rate falls with Prime, and the payment subsequently adjusts downward. True North Mortgage Commitment

You also have the ability to lock into a then-available 5-year fixed or closed-variable term. True North Mortgage Commitment

SBI

The rate also automatically changes with SBI's Prime. SBI Mortgage Commitment

But the commitment's stated mechanism means that falling rates can primarily reduce the amortization period unless you adjust the payment.

So:

Think gives you a more transparent "payment follows Prime" structure.

SBI gives you more of a "payment stays put, amortization changes" structure.

Neither is inherently universally better, but I personally consider Think's structure easier to understand and less likely to produce surprises.

So if there's even a moderate possibility that you'll move, refinance, or make large irregular payments, I would put considerably more weight on the contractual flexibility than on the 0.15% rate difference.
