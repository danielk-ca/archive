Solicitor's Act Assessment Officer: Fees from Lawyer getting reassessed - How much does it cost?
In Ontario, the initial upfront filing cost to have a lawyer’s bill assessed under the Solicitors Act is relatively low, but the total cost can become very expensive if you lose the hearing or hire legal representation. 
Upfront Court Filing Fees
According to the official Ontario Civil Court Fees, the administrative price depends entirely on who requests the assessment: 
  • $105.00 CAD if the assessment order is obtained by the client.
  • $194.00 CAD if the assessment order is obtained by the solicitor (lawyer). 
Note: If you missed the strict one-month deadline from receiving the invoice to request an assessment, you must first file a motion to get permission from a Superior Court judge, which introduces an additional motion filing fee of $194.00. 
Hidden & Potential Costs
While the entry fee is low, you need to be aware of the "loser pays" principle and other hidden financial factors involved in a formal assessment hearing: 
  • The "One-Sixth" Rule (Hearing Costs): Under Ontario law, if the Assessment Officer reduces your lawyer’s bill by less than one-sixth (16.6%), you will usually be ordered to pay the lawyer's legal costs for attending and defending the assessment. Conversely, if the bill is reduced by more than one-sixth, the lawyer may have to pay your costs. 
  • Adverse Cost Awards: Legal fee assessments function like standard court proceedings. If you lose or are deemed unreasonable, the Assessment Officer has the full discretion to award thousands of dollars in "costs" against you to cover the other side’s legal time. 
  • New Legal Fees: You do not legally require a lawyer to represent you at an assessment hearing. However, if you choose to hire a new lawyer or assessment advocate to argue your case, you will have to pay their regular hourly rates out of pocket.
